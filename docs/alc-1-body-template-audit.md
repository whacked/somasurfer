`scanBodyTemplateFolds` is the gate and `auditBodyTemplate` is not. The
per-level audit asks only whether a level violates its own two bisector
planes, which 162 of 3 228 templates across the parameter box satisfy while
still folding — a distant level can claim the point on the convex side of a
curve. The audit is reported here because its per-level margins are the right
tool for *localising* a fold, and for no other reason.

## 2. Regions the scan could not measure

**None.** All 1800 rays — 25 levels x 72 azimuths — reached the skin
mesh, so no azimuth is carrying a one-sided bound and every radius above is a
measurement rather than an upper estimate.

If a ray ever fails, the pipeline fills that azimuth with the largest radius
measured at the level — an **upper** bound — records it here as `UNVERIFIED`,
and the template is then sound *under that bound*, which is a weaker claim and
is published as one. The direction is the point: utilisation rises with radius,
so over-estimating makes the fold test strictly harder to pass. Filling with a
neighbour's radius, or with the level's minimum, would make an unmeasurable
region read green.

### `surface-is-limb`

At the listed levels and azimuths, the first skin crossing is a limb surface, not the trunk surface, so `r = 1` there is the outside of the arm or thigh.

- **why** A limb lying against the trunk leaves no skin between them, so there is no trunk surface to find along those rays. The radius is the honest distance to the skin and the frame stays exactly invertible; only the anatomical reading of `r = 1` changes.
- **how it is detected** A ray is flagged when it crosses the humerus or the femur before reaching the skin, then the witnessed azimuths are dilated by 3 knot(s) either way (15 degrees).
- **completeness** SOUND, NOT COMPLETE. A ray through limb soft tissue only, lateral or anterior to the bone, is not witnessed, so the published arc is a lower bound on the affected azimuths.
- **what would remove it** Needs a trunk-only surface. BodyParts3D publishes one skin concept for the whole body, so this source cannot supply it; a different surface source would remove the declaration without changing any address already issued.

### `single-sacral-level`

The whole fused sacrum is one addressable level, 133.5 mm tall against a 25.9 mm median — 5.2x. Sacral addressing is correspondingly coarse in the axial direction.

- **why** docs/alc-1-admissibility.md §2 measured the alternative: cutting the fused sacrum into short levels is strictly worse at every girth, because each cut both shortens the chord and tightens the turn, and the sacral concavity faces the deep pelvis where there is a lot of tissue. Five sacral levels put 1.2-3.2% of body volume inside a fold.
- **how it is detected** Structural. S02-S05 are reserved grammar and are realised by no template.
- **completeness** Exact. This is a property of the level set, not an estimate.
- **what would remove it** Not a defect to fix. A finer sacral frame can be added later without a breaking change because S02-S05 are reserved, but it needs the non-tubular pelvic parameterisation rather than more slabs of the same kind.

## 8. Precision, derived rather than chosen

`maxUsefulDigits` is **2**, and the `BD` frame itself caps at 12.

Rule: largest d with min(level height, clock-sector arc at the skin, radial half) / 2^d >= the skin mesh mean edge length.

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| medianLevelHeightMm | 25.88 |
| shortestLevelHeightMm | 14.46 |
| medianMaxRadiusMm | 159.83 |
| largestRadiusMm | 245.41 |
| clockSectorArcMm | 83.69 |
| radialHalfMm | 79.92 |
| smallestCellSideMm | 25.88 |
| skinMeanEdgeMm | 6.253 |
| azimuthKnots | 72 |
| azimuthKnotSpacingMm | 13.95 |

This is a property of the **source mesh**, not of the frame. The 99%-reduced
OBJ set has a 6.25 mm mean edge, and one octree digit past that the address
claims a distinction the geometry cannot make. A finer source raises this
number without invalidating any address already issued, because truncating an
address is always valid and the cells only ever subdivide.

Consumers must take the boundary from the **bound template's** own
`maxUsefulDigits` rather than from a literal. `locate()` raises
`flags.overPrecise` relative to whichever template is bound, so a harness that
hard-codes a digit count is pinned to one template and inverts against another.

The PC distance is `null` and that is deliberate. The distribution ships images
and masks, not landmark coordinates, and locating the posterior commissure in an
image is a segmentation problem rather than a header read. **No field of this
template is a function of that distance** — the frame takes the AC as its origin
and the AC–PC line as its anterior axis, and both of those are measured. A
plausible value would be the only unmeasured number in the file, and
`tools/verify-asset-templates.mjs` fails if an `UNVERIFIED` landmark ever
acquires one.
