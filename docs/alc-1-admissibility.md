# ALC-1 body template admissibility — measured report

Generated by `packages/alc/test/admissibility.sweep.mjs`. Every number below
is computed, not asserted. Regenerate it whenever the frame or a template
changes.

The `BD` frame is a tubular neighbourhood of the spinal curve, so it folds
where the body is thicker than the distance at which a level's two bounding
bisector planes meet. Beyond that fold two different addresses denote the
same millimetre point, and `encode(locate(a))` stops returning `a`.

**Utilisation** below is `bodyRadius / foldRadius` at the worst azimuth of
the worst level. Under 1 is admissible; 1 or more means part of the body is
ambiguous.

One level is better at every girth, and the ordering is monotone: cutting

the deep pelvis where there is a lot of tissue.

## 3. Where a collapsed sacral level takes its axis

With one sacral level, the binding site moves to the L5/S1 kink. How sharp
that kink is depends on where along the sacral curve the level takes its
axis direction: `0.0` follows the upper (S1) endplate, `0.5` is the chord of
the whole sacrum.

The chord convention folds a large body; the endplate convention does not
fold any body in this range. Note what the binding level becomes once the
kink is relieved: a mid-lumbar level, posteriorly, at around half
utilisation — which is as much margin as this frame can have.

## 4. Why the criterion has to be directional

The superseded criterion compared the *largest* radius at a level against
the radius of curvature. That assumes the thickest tissue faces the concave
side. In a trunk it faces the other way, because the spinal canal sits far
posterior.

| template | worst-case verdict | exact verdict | observed failures | false alarms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `anat-adult-p50` | pass | pass | 0 | - |
| `anat-adult-hyperlordotic` | FAIL L01,L02,L03,L04,L05 | pass | 0 | L01,L02,L03,L04,L05 |
| `anat-adult-large-girth` | FAIL L01,L02,L03,L04,L05 | pass | 0 | L01,L02,L03,L04,L05 |
| `anat-adult-hyperkyphotic` | pass | pass | 0 | - |
| `anat-child-7y` | pass | pass | 0 | - |
| `anat-adult-p50-split-sacrum` | FAIL S01,S02,S03,S04,S05 | FAIL S02,S03,S04 | 45 | S01,S05 |

The exact verdict matches the observed failures in every row. The worst-case
verdict condemns five lumbar levels of an ordinary wide-waisted adult whose
skin round-trips perfectly at every azimuth.

## 5. Requirements this puts on the asset pipeline

1. A body template realises **one** addressable sacral level, `S01`,
   covering the whole fused sacrum. `S02`-`S05` stay reserved in the
   grammar and absent from templates, which `locate()` already reports as
   `homology: 'absent'` rather than guessing.
2. That level's axis direction follows the **upper sacral endplate**, not
   the sacrum's chord.
3. `auditBodyTemplate()` must pass, and its per-level table must be
   published with the template. CI gates on it.
4. Templates also run `measureRoundTrip()`; the audit is analytic, the
   probe is the thing that cannot be fooled.

Requirements 1 and 2 cannot be retrofitted: changing the level set after
addresses are issued invalidates them.

Caveat: the geometry here is typical adult proportions, good to perhaps
10-15%, not a specific published cohort. It is sufficient for the questions
answered above — which direction binds, and by how much margin — and it is
no substitute for running the audit on the real mesh-derived template.
